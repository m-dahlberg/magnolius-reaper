SATURATOR

Adds harmonics in two independent flavours. Symmetric adds odd
harmonics (3rd, 5th, 7th): it rounds peaks evenly and sounds denser
and louder. Asymmetric adds even harmonics (2nd, 4th): it warms and
thickens, the tube or transformer colour. Turn up one, the other or
both, and pick the colour yourself instead of choosing a preset curve.

With every amount at 0 % the plugin is transparent, so start there
and bring things in until you hear them.

DRIVE

  Input       -18 to +18 dB. How hard the signal hits the curves.
              The main way to push harder. With Auto Gain on, it
              changes the amount of saturation, not the level.
  Slew        0 to 100 %. Softens fast, loud changes: transients
              and loud high frequencies round off, like an op-amp
              or tape running out of speed. Quiet material and low
              frequencies pass untouched. It runs first, so the
              level stages below never see the sharpest edges.
  IN meter    The level after Input, i.e. what drives the curves.
              -60 to +6 dBFS with a peak-hold line.

COLOUR

  Asymmetric  0 to 100 %. Amount of even-harmonic saturation.
  Symmetric   0 to 100 %. Amount of odd-harmonic saturation.
  Knee        Where the Symmetric curve starts to bend.
              Soft    saturates early and gently.
              Medium  in between (the default).
              Hard    stays clean, then bends near full scale.
              All three have the same level for quiet signals and
              only add odd harmonics; Knee changes the feel, not
              the colour. Switching crossfades, so it can be
              automated without clicks. At heavy settings Hard
              bends the most once it gets there.

OUTPUT

  Mix         0 to 100 %. Parallel saturation: blends the dry
              signal back in, lined up in time so nothing combs.
  Output      -18 to +18 dB. Final level trim.
  Auto Gain   On by default. Compensates the level change from
              Input and the colour controls, so you judge the
              colour and not the loudness. It is a fixed table
              (measured on pink noise at -18 dBFS RMS), not an
              envelope follower: within about 1 dB at sensible
              levels, less exact on very loud or very quiet
              material.
  Delta       Output level minus input level over the last 300 ms.
              Near 0 dB means Auto Gain is holding the level.
  OUT meter   The final output level.

HEADER

  Oversampling  1x, 2x or 4x. Runs the saturation at a higher rate
              so its harmonics do not fold back as harsh, unrelated
              tones (aliasing). 2x is the default and is clean at
              any setting; 4x is cleaner still. 1x uses no extra CPU
              and adds no latency, but it is for gentle settings
              only and loses a little top end when driven.
              Latency: 0 / 43 / 46 samples, compensated by REAPER.
              Changing it may click once: set it and leave it.
  Scale       Interface size. Fit follows the window.

QUICK CHECKS

  * All amounts at 0 %, Mix 100 %, Input and Output 0 dB: the
    output is the input.
  * Symmetric alone on a sine adds only odd harmonics; Asymmetric
    alone gives a strong 2nd harmonic.
  * Slew at 100 % takes a lot off a loud 10 kHz tone and almost
    nothing off a loud 1 kHz tone.

WHEN SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG

  * "images failed to load" in the header: the gui_kit folder is
    missing next to the plugin. Reinstall it with ReaPack.
  * It sounds louder or quieter than bypass at extreme settings:
    expected, Auto Gain is a fixed compensation. Use Output.
  * A thud or click when changing Oversampling: expected, the
    oversampler restarts.
