CONVOLUTION REVERB  (Magnolius)

True-stereo convolution reverb. A 4-channel impulse response is run
through uniform partitioned FFT convolution, so a room recorded in
true stereo keeps its cross-feed instead of collapsing to two
independent channels.

The reverb is active whenever a valid IR is loaded. Wet and Dry set
the balance; there is no separate bypass.


SETUP - DO THIS FIRST

1. Create the folder REAPER/Data/ReverbIRs and put your impulse
   responses in it. On Linux that is usually
   ~/.config/REAPER/Data/ReverbIRs. Subfolders are supported and are
   the tidy way to keep a library.

2. Pick an IR. Two sources, and the last one you touch wins:

   - The Reverb IR parameter in REAPER's parameter list. It is a file
     slider: REAPER owns it, it recurses into subfolders on its own,
     it is automatable, and it needs no setup. It stays visible on
     purpose - only the host can drive a file slider.

   - The browser in this window. It needs an index file (see below),
     but it gives you search, folders and counts.

3. Balance Wet and Dry. Both go down to -60 dB, which is off.

Accepted IR files:

   4-channel WAV  true stereo, channel order LL LR RL RR, with the
                  input letter first: ch1 = left in to left out,
                  ch2 = left to right, ch3 = right to left,
                  ch4 = right to right.
   2-channel WAV  parallel stereo (LL, RR), no cross-feed.
   mono WAV       the same IR on both channels.

Any sample rate works - IRs are resampled to the project rate when
they load. They are capped at 393216 samples (about 8.2 s at 48 kHz,
8.9 s at 44.1 kHz) and energy-normalised, so swapping IRs keeps a
comparable wet level. Past that the convolver drops the rest of the
tail, and the line above the decay view says so and gives both
figures.


THE IR BROWSER  (the upper panel)

Search box   Type anywhere in the window to filter - you do not have
             to click the box first. Clicking it lights its edges and
             shows a caret, which is simply where your typing is
             going. Terms are separated by spaces, all of them must
             match, and case is ignored. Backspace erases, Esc
             clears, and the small x at the right of the box clears
             it too.
Folder list  Click a folder to narrow the results to it. "All" shows
             everything. The number after each name is its file count.
File list    Click a file to load it. The green bar marks the IR that
             is loaded now. The mouse wheel scrolls either list.
Rescan       Re-reads the index file after you have added IRs.

Keys: Up and Down move the highlight, PgUp and PgDn move a page,
Enter loads the highlighted file.

The browser reads Data/ConvReverbIRs.idx, a plain text file with one
IR path per line, relative to Data/ReverbIRs. JSFX cannot list a
directory, so this file has to be generated: run tools/index_irs.py,
or the ReaScript tools/RescanReverbIRs.lua from inside REAPER, then
press Rescan. Without it the browser says so and the Reverb IR
parameter keeps working.

The browser stores its choice as a path, not as a position in the
list, so adding IRs later never repoints a saved project at some
other file.


THE DECAY VIEW  (the middle panel)

The line above it gives the channel count and the length of the IR
after the IR length stretch, so it moves as you move that knob - and
it keeps moving while you drag, ahead of the resample itself. It
also says which source the IR came from, browser or slider, and when
an IR is too long for the convolver it gives the file's real length
and how much of it is actually used.

The display is the peak envelope of the loaded IR, drawn as the
square root of amplitude. Linear would bury everything past the first
tenth of the tail; a decibel scale lifts the whole decay into the top
of the box and turns it into one solid slab. This keeps the wedge.
The dim band at the left, up to the bright line, is the pre-delay:
wet-path silence before the IR starts.

The ruler along the bottom is seconds from the moment the dry signal
hits, and it is a FIXED five-second window - it never rescales. So a
longer IR is drawn longer: move IR length and the envelope itself
stretches or shrinks against a ruler that stays put, which is what
you want to see. A tail running past five seconds is simply cut off
at the right edge; the length in seconds above the display is the
real figure either way.

Use it to see how long a tail really is, where the early reflections
sit, and how far IR length and pre-delay have moved things.


SHAPE

Pre-delay  0 to 500 ms. Delays only the wet path, so the dry hit
           stays where it is and the reverb arrives after it. Around
           80-150 ms on a percussive part keeps the transient clear.
           This is NOT latency and does not change reported PDC. A
           change equal-power crossfades over 512 samples instead of
           jumping the read pointer, which would pitch the tail.

Length     25 to 400 %. Resamples the IR, so it stretches or shortens
           the tail and shifts its spectrum with it, the way playing
           tape slower does. Longer is also darker; shorter is
           brighter. That is the character, not a fault. It re-reads
           nothing from disk, only re-resamples and re-transforms what
           is already loaded.

           Rebuilding costs more than one audio block, so the resample
           happens when you LET GO, not while you drag. The display
           does not wait: the percentage, the length in seconds and
           the decay view all follow your hand the whole way, because
           stretching is a pure time scaling and the length you are
           heading for is known exactly. Only what you HEAR waits for
           mouse up.


FILTER

High-pass  20 to 2000 Hz, 12 dB/oct, 20 Hz reads Off.
Low-pass   200 to 20000 Hz, 12 dB/oct, 20000 Hz reads Off.

Both act on the WET signal only - the dry path is never filtered.
They are 2-pole Butterworth biquads, always clamped below Nyquist,
and their cutoffs glide over about 30 ms, so moving a knob during
playback sweeps rather than steps.

The knobs run on a logarithmic taper so the useful low end is not
squeezed into the first tenth of the travel. The value fields under
them are in Hz either way, and so is automation.


MIX

Wet  -60 to +12 dB. -60 is off.
Dry  -60 to +12 dB. -60 is off.

The dry path is delayed internally to stay sample-aligned with the
wet path, so the two never smear against each other.


MODULATION

A convolution reverb is a fixed filter, so its tail is frozen - the
same comb structure on every note. On pads, strings, vocals and
cymbal wash that reads as a static, slightly glassy quality that
algorithmic hardware does not have. This adds slow movement to the
tail only. The early reflections, which carry where the source is
and what the room is, are left alone.

Depth  0 to 100 %. 0 is off, and then the output is exactly what it
       would be without this feature at all. At low settings the
       tail breathes: smooth, periodic, no audible pitch wobble. At
       high settings the movement turns random and you get obvious
       shimmer and diffusion on long tails. One control does both -
       Depth drives the sine-to-random blend as well as how far the
       taps move.
Rate   0.05 to 2 Hz. How fast. Slower than feels right is usually
       correct; the default sits near the bottom on purpose.
Onset  Where "late" begins, in ms from the start of the IR file.
       Leave it at 0 and the plugin works it out per IR - the field
       then shows what it chose. A ? after the number means the two
       detectors disagreed, or that it fell back to a default, so
       that one is worth a listen. n/a means this IR is not being
       modulated at all.

The onset is measured, not guessed at: the plugin looks for the
point where countable reflections give way to a diffuse wash. That
runs from about 10 ms in a small wooden room to 150 ms in a
cathedral, so one fixed number would be wrong for most of a library.

Modulation switches itself off for IRs under half a second. There is
not enough diffuse tail to be worth moving and too much early
structure to risk - which also keeps guitar cabinet impulses and
speaker correction curves out of it, since phase movement destroys
exactly what those encode.

Worth knowing: this moves phase, not level. The tail does not get
louder, quieter or duller when you turn Depth up. If you want to
hear what it is doing, use a sustained pad and A/B Depth at 0
against Depth at 25.

Modulation is deterministic - the same project bounces to the same
audio every time.


WORKING WITH THE CONTROLS

Every knob: drag up and down to change it, hold Shift for fine,
Ctrl-click to return it to its default, or use the wheel over it.

Every value field under a knob: drag it like a knob, or click once
and type a number. Enter commits, Esc cancels, clicking elsewhere
commits. Typing needs this FX window focused.

The scale menu in the title bar sizes the whole interface. It is
shared by every Magnolius plugin, so setting it here sets it
everywhere. A JSFX cannot resize its own window, so a scale larger
than the window will clip - drag the FX window bigger first, or use
Fit, which tracks whatever size REAPER gives it.


LATENCY

One partition, 2048 samples, reported through PDC while an IR is
loaded. REAPER compensates it automatically, offline renders
included. Pre-delay is not part of it.


WHEN SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG

No sound, or the dry signal only
  No valid IR is loaded. The line above the decay view says so. Check
  that Data/ReverbIRs has a 1, 2 or 4 channel WAV in it, and that Wet
  is not at -60.

The browser says no IR index found
  Data/ConvReverbIRs.idx does not exist yet. Generate it with
  tools/index_irs.py or the ReaScript, then press Rescan. Note that
  the index lives in Data/ and not inside Data/ReverbIRs, because
  anything in that folder also turns up in the file slider.

A red "images failed to load" line across the title bar
  The gui_kit folder is not next to the plugin as REAPER sees it. If
  the .jsfx is symlinked into the Effects folder, gui_kit has to be
  reachable from there too.

The tail sounds shorter than the IR
  An IR longer than about 8.2 s at 48 kHz, or stretched past that by
  IR length, has its tail dropped by the convolver. The line above
  the decay view says so, and gives both the real length and the part
  being used. Note this is NOT the same as the decay view's five
  second window: that one only clips the picture, silently, and never
  affects what you hear.

A click when switching IRs during playback
  Loading an IR re-reads the file and re-transforms it on the audio
  thread. Every convolver does this. Switch between takes, not
  mid-phrase.
