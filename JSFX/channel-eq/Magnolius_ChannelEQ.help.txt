CHANNEL EQ

A channel-strip equaliser: an 18 dB/oct high-pass, a 12 dB/oct
low-pass and four bands - LF shelf, LMF bell, HMF bell, HF shelf.
It is modelled from the transfer functions of a console-style
circuit, so it behaves like one: shelf corners move as you add
gain, cut is the exact mirror of boost, and the bells get
narrower as you push them harder.

Zero latency. No lookahead, no PDC. Mono or stereo, both channels
always get the same settings.


GETTING SET UP

1. Put it on a track. Everything starts neutral: the output is
   identical to the input until you move something.
2. Turn the gain knob of a band, then sweep its frequency knob.
   Watch the response curve move.
3. EQ In switches the four bands in and out, Filters In does the
   same for the high-pass and low-pass. Use them to A/B.


THE PANELS (left to right)

FILTERS (green)
  Upper knob  High-pass. Fully counter-clockwise is OUT. The
              numbers are in Hz.
  Lower knob  Low-pass. Fully clockwise is OUT. The numbers
              are in kHz. Frequency rises clockwise.
  Filters In  Switches both filters in and out together.
  EQ In       Switches the four bands in and out together.

LF (shelf)
  Gain        Boost to the right, cut to the left, 0 at the top.
  Freq        Corner, 30 to 450 Hz.

LMF (bell)
  Gain        As above.
  Freq        Centre, 0.2 to 2.5 kHz.
  Q           Width. The numbers run from 0.5 (wide) to 3
              (narrow).
  / 3         Moves the whole frequency range down, to about a
              quarter. The numbers on the knob then read four
              times too high.

HMF (bell)
  Gain, Freq  Centre, 0.6 to 7 kHz.
  Q           As above.
  x 3         Moves the whole frequency range up by about three.
              The numbers on the knob then read three times too
              low.

HF (shelf)
  Gain        As above.
  Freq        Corner, 1.5 to 16 kHz. The corner moves up as you
              add gain, so a big boost starts higher than the
              printed number. There is also a small dip just
              below the corner at full boost. Both are part of
              the circuit.

SATURATION (orange)
  Amount, Console / Tube. These are placeholders for now: they
  are saved and can be automated but they do not change the
  sound yet.


THE NUMBERS

The numbers around each knob are spread evenly round its sweep.
The knob follows them: the pointer on a printed number gives that
frequency (or Q), and between two numbers it is smooth. The numbers
are the panel's nominal values, not a calibration: the real centre
or corner follows the circuit and can differ from the printed one
by up to about a fifth (more for a strongly boosted shelf). Trust
the response curve and your ears.

The gain knobs show only - 0 +. How many dB a turn gives depends on
the Gain law (header):
  Circuit    The way the original behaves. The shelves keep most
             of their range for the last fifth of the turn.
  dB-linear  Equal dB per turn. Full travel is about +-20 dB (HF),
             +-21 dB (bells) and +-18 dB (LF).
Cut always mirrors boost exactly.


THE RESPONSE CURVE

Bright line: the whole chain. Dim lines: each band on its own.
20 Hz to 20 kHz, +-24 dB. The Curve button hides it and the panel
becomes shorter. The window itself keeps its size; use the scale
menu (Fit, 100 % ... 200 %) to fill it.

Ctrl-click a knob to reset it. Hold Shift while dragging for fine
control. The mouse wheel works too.


IF SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG

- The header says "images failed to load": the gui_kit folder is
  missing next to the plugin (see the README).
- A knob moves but nothing changes: check EQ In and Filters In,
  and that a band's gain is not at 0.
- Changes take up to 5 ms to start and then glide for about 30 ms.
  That is deliberate: it stops zipper noise when you sweep.
